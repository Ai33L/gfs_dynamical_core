# JAX Dynamical Core Debugging Log - Phase 1

This log documents the systematic debugging process undertaken to identify the root cause of the JAX dynamical core blow-up (where surface pressure diverges steadily by step 30).

## 1. Initial State Investigation (Stage 0)

I began by running `examples/compare_debug_dumps.py` to compare the JAX model state against Fortran at every Runge-Kutta stage. 

**Observation:** Right at Stage 0 (initial conditions, after a single grid $\rightarrow$ spectral $\rightarrow$ grid roundtrip), the script reported a massive error in the eastward wind (`u`) between JAX and Fortran:
- JAX vs True IC error: ~0.05 m/s
- Fortran vs True IC error: ~1.0 m/s

**Hypothesis:** Either Fortran or JAX has a fundamental flaw in its vector transform (`getvrtdivspec` vs `s2fft.forward_jax`).

## 2. Uncovering the Validation Script Flaw

I wrote a test script (`test_error_location.py`) to locate where the 1.0 m/s error was occurring in the Fortran output. The error was concentrated near the mid-latitudes.

However, I discovered a critical bug in the validation script itself (`compare_debug_dumps.py`). The script assumed Fortran output latitudes from South-to-North and was manually flipping the array (`[::-1]`). I verified that `climt` feeds a North-to-South grid into the model, and Fortran outputs it natively as North-to-South. 

**Fix:** By removing the erroneous latitude flip, the Fortran vs True IC error dropped from ~1.0 m/s to ~0.62 m/s. The previous 1.0 m/s error was caused by comparing the Northern Hemisphere jet against the Southern Hemisphere!

## 3. The 0.62 m/s Discrepancy & Scaling Issue

Even after fixing the latitude flip, Fortran still had a 0.62 m/s discrepancy compared to the raw initial conditions, while JAX only had 0.05 m/s. 

I extracted the zonal mean profile of this error (`test_scaling.py`) and found that the Fortran wind profile matches the initial wind profile perfectly in shape, but is systematically **scaled** by a factor (mean ratio ~1.019).

This indicates a structural difference in how SHTNS and `s2fft` handle the normalisation or mathematical formulation of the vector spherical harmonics. 

## 4. Ruling out the Condon-Shortley Phase (Red Herring)

I hypothesized that the `0.62` m/s error was caused by a mismatch in the Condon-Shortley phase convention. SHTNS is explicitly initialized with `SHT_NO_CS_PHASE` in the Fortran code, while `s2fft` includes it by default. 

**Investigation:**
1. I compared the Fortran and JAX spectral coefficients for the $m=1$ mode and found they indeed had opposite signs.
2. I wrote a script (`test_cs_correction.py`) to manually apply the $(-1)^m$ correction to the JAX spectral state to make it match Fortran.

**Conclusion:** The correction did **not** resolve the grid-space error. In fact, it made it worse. 
Why? Because I discovered that the absolute magnitude of the $m=1$ mode was only `~1.49e-08`. A sign flip on a coefficient this small cannot explain a `0.62` m/s difference in the physical wind field. 

Furthermore, because the forward and inverse transforms in both libraries are perfectly symmetric with respect to their own internal CS phase conventions, the phase difference cancels out during a round-trip. The Condon-Shortley phase is just an internal representational choice and does not affect the physical grid outputs.

## 5. Isolating the Error to the Zonal Mean ($m=0$)

Realizing the error was macroscopic (~0.62 m/s), I decomposed the `u` wind field into its zonal mean ($m=0$) and non-zonal ($m \neq 0$) components (`test_zonal_diff.py`).

**Investigation & Findings:**
- The non-zonal perturbation ($m \neq 0$) matches between JAX and Fortran to machine precision (`1.36e-13`).
- The entire `0.62` m/s discrepancy is completely isolated to the zonal mean flow ($m=0$).

This is a massive breakthrough. It proves that the spin-weighted vector transforms are perfectly mathematically compatible for all wave-like features. The bug lies purely in how the $m=0$ mode is handled during the vector transforms. 

There are two possibilities for this zonal mean divergence:
1. The forward transform generates different spectral coefficients for $m=0$.
2. The inverse transform scales the $m=0$ coefficients differently when projecting back to grid space.

## 6. Investigating Zonal Mean (m=0) Discrepancy

**Hypothesis:** The ~0.62 m/s error in the initial JAX `u` wind field (compared to Fortran) is entirely contained within the $m=0$ (zonal mean) mode. This could be due to a fundamental difference in how `s2fft` and `shtns` perform the numerical integration for the forward transform (grid -> spectral) of the $m=0$ mode, potentially due to differing Gaussian quadrature weights.

**Test:** I will extract the Gaussian quadrature weights (`gauwts`) directly from the Fortran `shtns` module and compare them to the weights generated by `numpy.polynomial.legendre.leggauss` (which JAX/`s2fft` uses). If they differ, it explains the discrepancy in the forward integral.

## 8. Analyzing Zonal Mean (m=0) Behavior at the Poles

**Hypothesis:** The large 0.62 m/s error in Fortran's zonal mean wind might be caused by the initial wind field  being non-zero at the poles. A non-zero zonal mean (=0$) wind at the poles is mathematically discontinuous. If Fortran SHTNS forces this discontinuous field to be represented by vector spherical harmonics (which strictly go to 0 at the poles for =0$) while also swapping the $ and $ components, it could trigger massive Gibbs ringing globally.

**Test:** I will examine the zonal mean of , , and  specifically at the poles and plot the profile to see if Gibbs ringing is present.

## 9. Claude's Assessment (2026-03-23) — Vector Transform m=0 as Root Cause

**Context:** After exhaustive line-by-line verification of ALL dycore equations (PGF, vertical velocities, semi-implicit matrices, diffusion, Coriolis, linear tendencies, implicit solve) across two sessions, no bugs were found. The CS phase hypothesis was confirmed as a red herring (section 4). The only unexplained discrepancy is the 0.62 m/s zonal-mean (m=0) error from section 5.

**Assessment:** The ~2% systematic scaling error in the m=0 zonal wind after a grid→spectral→grid roundtrip is the most likely root cause. This error in the background jet gets amplified through nonlinear vorticity-divergence interactions over 7-8 simulated days until blow-up. All other components have been verified correct.

**Current investigation:** Running `test_synthetic_sandbox.py` to compare SHTNS vs s2fft vector decomposition on simple analytic wind profiles (cos(lat), sin(lat), wave patterns). This will identify exactly where the two libraries diverge for m=0 vector modes.

## 10. Transform Equivalence Confirmed (2026-03-23, Session 2)

### 10.1 Forward transforms agree to machine precision

Ran `examples/test_synthetic_sandbox.py` with `zonal_u_cos_lat` case (u = 10·cos(lat), v = 0, T = 300 K uniform). Comparing the spectral coefficients produced by Fortran/SHTNS vs JAX/s2fft:

- **Vorticity**: max|diff| = 6.67e-19 (ratio = 1.0000 for all significant modes)
- **Divergence**: both ~zero (max ~5e-20), as expected for purely zonal flow
- **Temperature**: max|diff| = 1.13e-11 (ratio = 1.0000)

**Conclusion:** The forward vector transforms produce **identical** spectral coefficients. The two libraries agree perfectly for the grid→spectral direction.

### 10.2 s2fft roundtrip is exact

Tested s2fft roundtrip (forward + inverse) for u = 10·cos(lat):
- Without truncation: max|u_diff| = 4.72e-13, zm ratio = 1.0000000000
- With T40 truncation: max|u_diff| = 2.22e-13, zm ratio = 1.0000000000

The s2fft spin-1 vector transform is perfectly self-consistent. The roundtrip error is at machine precision.

### 10.3 The 0.62 m/s "error" is likely just truncation loss

The 0.62 m/s discrepancy reported in section 3 (Fortran vs raw IC) is the **truncation error** from representing the narrow JW06 jet at T40 resolution. The JW06 jet has significant spectral content above l=40, so truncation removes real signal.

The 0.05 m/s error for JAX (section 3) may have been measured at a time when JAX was not yet applying T40 truncation, or was using a different comparison methodology. Both libraries now truncate at T40 (`enforce_triangular_truncation` in `transforms.py`, line 289).

### 10.4 Fortran debug dump inconsistency

When feeding Fortran's spectral coefficients from `debug_data/fortran_step_1_stage_0.bin` to s2fft inverse (`test_m0_cross_library.py`):
- **Temperature (scalar)**: JAX inverse matches Fortran grid output to 1e-13 ✓
- **Wind (vector) m=0**: Only 0.036 m/s difference
- **Wind (vector) m>0**: 0.94 m/s eddy difference — BUT this is an artifact

The Fortran dump's spectral vorticity has essentially zero m>0 content (~1e-14 energy), yet the dumped grid winds show 0.94 m/s eddies. This is inconsistent. The most likely explanation is that the grid winds in the dump are the **original initial conditions** (before spectral transform), not the spectrally-reconstructed winds. The `dump_intermediate` routine in `run.f90` (lines 384-423) calls `getuv` before writing, but the dump file may be stale from an earlier compilation without this reconstruction code. Temperature is consistent because it was always written from spectral.

**Implication:** The cross-library vector comparison for m>0 is invalid with this dump. The m=0 result (0.036 m/s) may also be unreliable.

### 10.5 Revised assessment: transforms are NOT the root cause

Given that:
1. Forward transforms agree to 1e-19
2. s2fft roundtrip is exact (1e-13)
3. Both truncate at T40
4. The 0.62 m/s was truncation loss, not a normalization bug

**The vector transforms are correct.** The blow-up root cause must lie elsewhere — most likely in the dynamics equations (`dynamics.py`), the IMEX stepper (`stepper.py`), or the semi-implicit solver.

### 10.6 Next steps

The stepper and dynamics code have been read but not yet compared against Fortran execution. Key areas to investigate:
- **Pressure gradient force** (`compute_pressure_gradient_force`): complex multi-term accumulation, easy to get signs or indexing wrong
- **Vertical advection** (`compute_vertical_advection`): boundary conditions at surface/TOA
- **Semi-implicit IMEX stepper** (`advance` in `stepper.py`): the nonlinear/linear tendency splitting and implicit solve
- **Grid tendency assembly** (`assemble_grid_tendencies`): signs of u_flux/v_flux terms
- **Diffusion application**: the forward implicit damping at lines 686-702

The most direct approach is to run a single timestep with both Fortran and JAX, dump all intermediate fields (tendencies, pressure diagnostics, vertical velocities), and compare term-by-term to isolate which computation first diverges.

### 11.7 Gradient computation: potential discrepancy to investigate

Fortran computes gradients using `getgrad` (`shtns.f90` lines 374–413), which
calls `shtns_sphtor_to_spat` with the input as the "divergence" and zero as
the "vorticity":
```fortran
vrtspec_tmp = 0.
divspec_tmp = divspec   ! the scalar field
call shtns_sphtor_to_spat(vrtspec_tmp, divspec_tmp, ugrid, vgrid)
```

This uses the SHTNS vector synthesis to compute the gradient as a vector field.
The relationship is: `∇f = sphtor_to_spat(0, f_lm)` because the gradient of a
scalar can be expressed as a toroidal/poloidal decomposition where the toroidal
part (from vorticity=0) vanishes.

JAX computes gradients via spin-1 synthesis (`transforms.py` lines 128–131):
```python
F1_t_lm = -l_factor[:, None] * temp
f_t_spin1 = s2fft.inverse_jax(F1_t_lm, L, spin=1, sampling=sampling)
grad_t_x = f_t_spin1.imag / radius
grad_t_y = -f_t_spin1.real / radius
```

These should be mathematically equivalent (both compute the surface gradient
of a scalar field), but this has NOT been numerically verified by feeding the
same spectral coefficients to both and comparing grid output. The `getgrad`
function uses `sphtor_to_spat` (same as `getuv` but without the `invlap*R`
pre-factor), while JAX uses `-l_factor * f_lm` followed by spin-1 inverse.

**Status: NOT YET VERIFIED NUMERICALLY. This is a remaining suspect.**

### 12.5 Current hypothesis [CODE READING]

The 2% v error is caused by a difference in the semi-implicit linear
divergence tendency between Fortran and JAX. This tendency is:

```
ddivdtlin = -lap * (amhyb @ T_spectral + tor_hyb * lnps_spectral)
```

where `lap = -l(l+1)`, and `amhyb`, `tor_hyb` are pre-computed matrices
from the vertical coordinate.

By code reading, the matrix construction in JAX (`init_semi_implicit_matrices`
in `stepper.py` lines 112–247) appears structurally identical to Fortran's
`init_semimpdata` (`semimp_data.f90` lines 128–277):
- Same reference temperature (300 K) and pressure (800 hPa)
- Same `pkref`, `dpkref`, `alfaref` computation
- Same `yecm` (geopotential operator) and `tecm` (energy conversion operator)
- Same flip from TTB to BTU ordering
- Same `amhyb/R²`, `tor_hyb = Rd*Tref/R²`
- Same `ym = outer(tor_hyb, svhyb) + amhyb @ bmhyb`
- Same `d_hyb_m = inv(I + (c*dt)² * n(n+1) * ym)`

**Status: NOT YET VERIFIED NUMERICALLY.** The matrices have not been
compared element-by-element between Fortran and JAX. This is the next step.

### 15.7 PGF formula comparison — potential sentinel rlnp bug found (CODE READING)

Compared Fortran `getpresgrad` (dyn_run.f90:487-563) with JAX
`compute_pressure_gradient_force` (dynamics.py:260-344).

**Potential bug found:** The Fortran `cofa` computation at the top layer
(k=1, TOA) uses `rlnp(:,:,1) = 99999.99` (a sentinel value documented as
"should never be used"). But the `cofa` loop runs from k=1 to nlevs
WITHOUT special-casing k=1:

```fortran
do k=1,nlevs
   cofa(:,:,k)=-(1./dpk(:,:,k))*( &
    bk(k+1)*pk(:,:,k)/pk(:,:,k+1) - bk(k) &
   +rlnp(:,:,k)*( bk(k)-pk(:,:,k)*dbk(k)/dpk(:,:,k) )  )
enddo
```

At k=1: bk(1)=0, so the rlnp term = 99999.99 * (0 - pk(1)*dbk(1)/dpk(1))
≈ 99999.99 * (-0.000197) ≈ -19.7. This is a large erroneous contribution.

JAX guards against this by setting rlnp[-1]=0 at the top layer and using
`jnp.where(jnp.abs(cofa_coef) > 1e-15, ...)` guards.

However, this bug only affects the **top layer**, while the 2.2% error
affects ALL levels. So this may not be the primary cause. It needs
numerical verification.

### 18.2 Condon-Shortley phase convention mismatch [CODE READING]

**Root cause of comparison artifact:** s2fft includes the Condon-Shortley
(CS) phase by default, while SHTNS was initialized with `SHT_NO_CS_PHASE`.
This means spectral coefficients differ by a factor of `(-1)^m`:

    f_lm^{SHTNS} = (-1)^m × f_lm^{s2fft}

For **even m**: coefficients are identical → comparison correct.
For **odd m**: coefficients have **opposite sign** → `max|F-J| = 2×max|F|`,
giving 200% apparent relative error.

The `s2fft_to_packed` conversion in `compare_full_run.py` extracted raw
s2fft coefficients without applying the `(-1)^m` correction.

### 18.4 Impact on earlier findings

The CS phase is an **internal representational convention** that cancels
in forward/inverse transform pairs. It does NOT affect the dynamics:

- All spectral operations (Laplacian, diffusion, semi-implicit) depend
  only on `l`, not `m`, so the `(-1)^m` factor passes through unchanged.
- Forward transform followed by inverse transform always recovers the
  correct grid field regardless of CS convention.

**Therefore:**
- §15.3's 2.2% tendency error (measured at m=0) is **real** and unaffected
  by the CS artifact.
- §16.2's PGF comparison (grid space) is **real** and correct.
- §16.4's hypothesis that the 2.2% is roundoff now appears more plausible:
  the CS-corrected comparison shows **0.03% real error** after one step.

### 18.5 Revised assessment of the blow-up

With CS correction applied, the **real errors** at step 1 are:
- vrt: 1.8e-7 relative (essentially machine precision)
- div: 3.1e-3 relative (0.3%, on tiny absolute values)
- T: 3.1e-9 relative (essentially machine precision)
- lnps: 3.6e-10 relative (machine precision)

The JAX blow-up at day 7.15 cannot be caused by a 0.03% spectral error.
The blow-up occurs during the nonlinear wave-breaking phase of the
baroclinic instability. **Possible real causes:**

1. **Chaotic divergence**: tiny floating-point differences grow
   exponentially in the nonlinear regime, pushing JAX onto a trajectory
   that violates CFL while Fortran stays stable.
2. **Missing stabilization**: Fortran may have an implicit stabilization
   mechanism that JAX lacks (e.g., different dealiasing, or the
   `getvadv_tracers` flux limiter applied to momentum).
3. **Aliasing**: the 2/3 dealiasing rule may differ between SHTNS and
   s2fft quadrature.

## 2026-04-18 — Phase 1 dump alignment bug fixed (code-reading + build verification)

**Symptom.** The Fortran `getdyntend` dump file was 65024 bytes short of

...

pressure-dependent quantity: `dp`, `prs`, `alfa`, `rlnp`, the

**Status.** Bug identified, fix not applied. Proposed fix is a
one-line change in `component_jax.py:116` — read
`get_constant("top_of_model_pressure", "Pa")` the same way the

### 11k. ROOT CAUSE: reality-symmetry violation = undamped tangent-linear ghost mode [NUMERICAL TEST]

`check_reality_symmetry.py` measured `f(l,−m) − (−1)^m conj(f(l,+m))`
in the JAX cached spectral snapshots:

| step | div violation / scale |
|------|----------------------|
| 800  | 2.8e-08 |
| 1200 | 9.9e-06 |
| 1600 | 4.6e-03 |
| 2000 | **9.9e-02** |

Pure exponential, e-fold = 1200/ln(6.6e7) ≈ **66 steps = 5.5 h —
exactly the SH instability growth rate (§11i)**.

Mechanism: s2fft stores the full (l, ±m) rectangle and treats fields
as general complex functions. A real field requires
f(l,−m) = (−1)^m conj(f(l,+m)); SHTNS enforces this by construction
(stores m≥0 only, real synthesis) — the JAX pipeline never did.
Roundoff (mainly the dual-spin ± forward combinations) seeds a tiny
violation; the violation is exactly an imaginary-valued grid field,
which evolves as an **undamped tangent-linear perturbation** of the
flow. It rides the jet's fastest baroclinic modes (m=11–15: the SH
jet's most unstable waves), e-folds every ~5.5 h, and after ~34
e-folds (≈7.9 days from an O(1e-16) seed — matching the observed
blow-up window) feeds back into the real part through nonlinear terms
(exp(lnps), field products) and destroys the solution.

Explains every prior observation: IMEX and explicit both blow up
(shared representation); 2× diffusion only delays ~1 day (weak damping
of l≈30 modes barely dents σ); every map test passed (tests always fed
reality-symmetric states); restarts through grid fields reset Im→0 and
recover; Fortran (real arithmetic, m≥0 storage) is structurally immune.
